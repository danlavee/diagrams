# Composition

Opened from SKILL.md.

Composition places nodes so **every fact the picture must keep holding** can hold. Pathways get better because grouping moved.

A fact the picture must keep holding is a relationship on the lowest parent that contains the participants (or nesting). Domain meaning is not enough. How marks sit, occupy, meet, and are read is a picture-holding fact, owed whether or not the storyboard named it. Composition is not finished while a visible picture fact has no home.

A home is a relationship that grouping can discharge without leftover. Naming leftover is not a home.

Unless the story names an exception, preferred reading is left to right and top to bottom, and sequence and flow follow it. A mark does not occupy another mark's tip, label, or interior except at a joint. Peers that share a face or a line are placed on it. A path meets a face at its meeting, along the normal, at the face center. Several landings on one face are spaced on that face. A path ends on a meeting. A diagonal exists only when a relationship names it. Density that forces extra joints is unfinished composition. Those sentences are standing facts. Grouping must make them hold.

Leftover is geometry the standing facts forbid, or that grouping would have removed. If discharging a relationship would produce leftover, **re-enter composition**. Do not keep adding bends.

## How to use

1. Place groups from the storyboard so the preferred reading direction can hold.
2. Write every picture-holding relationship on the parent that owns those participants. Write a relationship when the story names an exception to a standing fact; otherwise grouping must make that fact hold.
3. If they cannot be discharged without leftover, return to step 1.
4. Paint only after they can be discharged onto marks.

The seeing pass looks at the marks. Standing facts still hold, or a relationship names the story's exception. Every other visible picture fact has a relationship. No leftover remains. If not, grouping or the ledger is incomplete — return to composition, not more routing.

## When the picture has paths

Same law. A same-path meeting is a joint; clearance from that joint to the next meeting is a relationship. A path meets a face at its meeting, along the normal, at the face center unless the story names another point; a vertex only when the story names it. Fan-out is one trunk then branches of the same path; fan-in is the reverse. The same path may join at a joint; distinct paths do not share a segment. Two paths at one point are a joint only if they are the same path; otherwise they cross or composition moves them — that choice is a relationship.
